# Instructions

You are about to participate in an experiment on decision making. The experiment is designed not to test your knowledge, but to learn about your decision making.

You will complete **4 trial rounds followed by 25 main rounds**. The practice rounds are designed to help you become familiar with the survey and **will not affect your payment**.

For completing the survey, you will receive a **base payment of HK$70.00**. You may also earn **additional bonus payments** based on your decisions in the 25 main rounds.

Your bonus will be based on **your average payoff across the 25 main rounds**. Each experimental dollar included in your payment equals one token, and each token you earn is worth **HK$12.00**. Your final payment will therefore consist of your **HK$70.00 base payment plus any bonus payment earned from the main rounds**.

## Your Task in Each Round

A firm offers a new product to a market of **4 consumers  (you and 3 other people in this room)**.

You remain with the same 3 other consumers throughout all 4 trial rounds and 25 main rounds. Your arrival order and conditions are randomly assigned again in each round.

- The 4 consumers arrive in a random order.
- Each consumer chooses **ORDER** or **DO NOT ORDER** once.
- Before deciding, you will see how many consumers arrived before you, how many ordered, and how many did not order.
- After all decisions are made, production is scheduled in one-week slots beginning at Week 0, following the order in which purchases were placed.
- Every round starts with a new product and a new **$4.00 endowment**.

## Product Value

The product has one value shared by all 4 consumers (including you) in that round:

- **50% probability:** high value of **$3.50**
- **50% probability:** low value of **$0.00**

Before considering information from the current round, the expected product value is:

**50% × $3.50 + 50% × $0.00 = $1.75**

The product value is drawn once per round. A new value is drawn in the next round.

## Consumer Type

In every round, the computer randomly assigns exactly 2 of the 4 consumers to be informed and the other 2 to be uninformed. Before this assignment, each consumer has a 50% chance of being informed.

- **50% probability: INFORMED.**  With a probability of 50%, a consumer (for example, you) is informed about the product value. An informed consumer will be told about the true product value ($0 or $3.50) before she places an order, and she can simply compare the true product value with the waiting cost.
- **50% probability: UNINFORMED.**  However, with a probability of 50%, a consumer (for example, you) is not informed about the product value. An uninformed consumer will not learn about the true product value until after you receive the product. All an uninformed consumer knows is that the product value is either $0 or $3.50 (with 50% probability each), i.e., she would expect the product value to be (50% \* $0) + (50% \* $3.50) = $1.75 (on average).

You will see your own information status before deciding. You will not see the information status of individual other consumers.

## Understanding the Wait Times

Before deciding, you will see:

- **Queuing time:** Production begins in Week 0. Each order requires one week to produce, and each order placed before yours adds one week before production of your own order begins. Therefore, your queuing time equals the number of earlier orders: 0–3 weeks.
- **Shipping time:** either 0 or 1 week. In each round, exactly 2 consumers have 0 shipping weeks and exactly 2 have 1 shipping week. Among the 2 informed consumers, one has 0 shipping weeks and one has 1 shipping week. The same applies to the 2 uninformed consumers. These conditions are randomly assigned again each round, separately from arrival order. Before assignment, each consumer has a 50% chance of each shipping time. Each consumer sees only their own shipping time before deciding.
- **Total wait time:** queuing time + shipping time.
- **Waiting cost:** total wait time × **$1.00**, paid only if you order.

For example, suppose your decision page shows: “3 consumers arrived before you: 1 ordered, 2 did not.” Your queuing time is 1 week: the earlier order is produced in the Week 0 slot and your order is produced in the Week 1 slot.

## Your Decision and Payoff

- **DO NOT ORDER:** You keep your $4.00 endowment.\
  **Payoff = $4.00**
- **ORDER:** You pay the waiting cost and receive the product value.\
  **Payoff = $4.00 − total waiting cost + product value**

Example: if total wait time is 2 weeks:

- Low-value product: **$4.00 − $2.00 + $0.00 = $2.00**
- High-value product: **$4.00 − $2.00 + $3.50 = $5.50**
In sum, you are a consumer participating in a repeated market experiment in which you decide in each round whether to order a product from a firm under uncertainty about its value and with a delay before delivery.
In each round, you are part of a fixed group of 4 consumers, and the experiment consists of 4 trial rounds followed by 25 main rounds. At the start of every round, the product's quality (high or low) is randomly determined and remains the same for all consumers in that round. Your consumer type (informed or uninformed) is also randomly assigned each round, and the probability of being informed and the prior probability of high quality are fixed parameters of the environment and common knowledge. You observe your own consumer type, but not the types of individual other consumers.
All consumers arrive in a random order. When you arrive, you observe your arrival rank, how many prior consumers chose to order, and how many chose not to order. You are quoted a total wait time equal to your queuing time plus your shipping time. Queuing time equals the number of prior consumers who chose to order. Shipping time is either 0 or 1 week and is assigned according to the rules of your experimental condition. Consumer types and shipping times are randomly assigned again each round, separately from arrival order, and you observe only your own shipping time. Each week of total wait time costs you $1 if you order. The firm is non-strategic and follows a fixed rule: after all decisions are made, it schedules production in one-week slots beginning at Week 0, following the order in which purchases were placed.
Queuing time and shipping time both contribute to your waiting cost, but they arise differently: queuing time reflects earlier consumers' order decisions, whereas shipping time is assigned by the experiment. Your arrival rank and the number of earlier orders together tell you how many earlier consumers chose not to order. Earlier consumers may have known the product's value, so both orders and non-orders may provide information about its quality. However, their decisions also depend on their own waiting costs and information status, which you do not observe individually. The information conveyed by earlier decisions therefore depends on the probability of being informed specified in your experimental condition.
Within each round, the sequence is: product quality, consumer types, and shipping times are realized → consumers arrive in random order → each consumer observes arrival rank, prior order and non-order counts, queuing time, shipping time, and total wait time, then makes a one-time decision to order or not → after all decisions, production and delivery occur. Decisions cannot be revised. The $4 endowment is reset each round. Trial rounds do not affect payment, and your bonus is based on your average payoff across the 25 main rounds. Product quality and consumer assignments are drawn anew each round.
