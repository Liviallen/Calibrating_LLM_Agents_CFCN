- **Manipulated variables and experimentally-varied design elements**
  - This is the **Q50 condition**, so in each round each subject has an individual **50% chance of being informed**.
  - The experiment also varies the **prior probability that product quality is high**; in Q50, the applicable prior for a given round is whatever is shown in that round’s instructions/screen (from the design, this can be **0.50 or 0.25**).
  - **Product quality** varies by round and is newly drawn each round as either **high** or **low**.
  - **Arrival order** varies by round through random sequencing of the four subjects.
  - The quoted **wait time** seen at decision depends on earlier purchase decisions in that round, so it is an endogenous decision input rather than a fixed product feature.

- **Role structure**
  - In each round there is **one firm** and a market of **4 subjects** as potential buyers.
  - All participants are in the same **buyer/consumer role**; there is no separate participant seller role.
  - A subject’s active decision in a round is simply **whether to purchase or not purchase** when called to decide.
  - “**Informed**” and “**uninformed**” are temporary round-specific information states, not permanent roles.

- **Information structure**
  - In Q50, whether a subject is informed is determined **separately for each subject in each round** and privately revealed to that subject by the interface/instructions.
  - An **informed** subject observes the realized product quality/value for that round before deciding.
  - An **uninformed** subject does **not** observe realized quality and knows only the stated prior probability and the general structural parameters.
  - The realized product quality is a **single common round-level state** shared by all four subjects in that round; informed subjects observe that same realized quality, while uninformed subjects do not.
  - All subjects know the structural parameters of the environment, including **market size, prior probability, probability of being informed, product values, and waiting cost**.
  - When deciding, subjects observe the **current quoted wait time** they would face if they buy.
  - Wait time is informative because it reflects **cumulative prior purchases**, not just production speed.
  - Subjects do **not** know their **rank in the arrival sequence** when deciding.
  - Because rank is unknown, a subject cannot infer exactly how many earlier subjects have already arrived and declined; they observe only the delivery delay they would face if they purchase.
  - Subjects have access to a **history** of past rounds, including prior wait times, information status/type, decisions, and realized product values.

- **Timing and sequence**
  - The experiment has **26 decision rounds**, each corresponding to a new product.
  - In each round, **product quality is drawn first**.
  - The four subjects then arrive **sequentially in a random order**.
  - When a subject arrives, they observe their **information status**, the **quoted wait time**, and, if informed, the realized **product quality/value**.
  - The subject then immediately chooses **purchase or no purchase**.
  - After all four decisions are made, the **production/delivery process is simulated**.
  - The firm can produce **exactly one unit per week**, and buyers are served **in order of purchase**.
  - A subject who buys at quoted wait time **w** waits exactly **w weeks/periods** for delivery in that round.
  - The post-decision delivery animation is only a **representation of the already determined queue outcome**, used to reinforce understanding.
  - After a round ends, the next round begins with a **new independent product** and a fresh set of round-specific draws.

- **Matching and memory**
  - Subjects are assigned to fixed **cohorts of 4** and remain in the **same cohort across all rounds** (partner design).
  - Even though cohorts remain fixed, each round is formally a **new independent product** with a new quality draw, new arrival order, and new informed-status realizations.
  - Past outcomes do **not mechanically carry over** into the next round’s queue or payoffs.
  - Because the history box remains visible, subjects can remember and potentially condition behavior on **past-round experiences**.

- **Incentives and payoffs**
  - Each round begins with a fresh **$4 endowment/budget**.
  - If the subject does **not** purchase, round payoff is **$4**.
  - If the subject **does** purchase, round payoff equals **$4 + product value − waiting cost**.
  - Product value is **$3.50 if quality is high** and **$0 if quality is low**.
  - Waiting cost is **$1 per week (per unit of wait time)**.
  - Thus, the payoff from purchasing depends on both **realized quality** and the **wait time at purchase**.
  - A subject’s payoff can depend indirectly on earlier subjects’ actions because earlier purchases affect the quoted wait time they face.
  - Final compensation is based on **average earnings across the 26 rounds**, so every round affects final payment.

- **Randomization and assignment**
  - In every round, **product quality** is randomly drawn according to the publicly known prior for that round.
  - In Q50, each subject’s **informed/uninformed status** is randomly assigned each round with **50% probability of being informed**.
  - **Arrival order** among the four subjects is randomly assigned each round.
  - A random delay is added before the first arrival so subjects cannot infer their rank from clock timing alone.
  - These stochastic elements—**quality, information status, and arrival order**—are redrawn anew each round even though cohort membership stays fixed.
