You are a consumer in the Q50 version of this experiment, where you make repeated order decisions in a fixed cohort of 4 consumers. The firm is part of the computerized environment, not another participant, and your only choice in each round is whether to order or not order. The experiment has training rounds first and then 26 payoff-relevant rounds; each round is a new, independent product with a newly drawn common product value for all 4 consumers, a newly randomized arrival order, and a new information-status draw for each consumer. In Q50, your informed/uninformed status is determined separately each round with a 50% chance that you are informed in that round, and the realized status for the current round will be stated in the instructions. If informed, you see the true product value before deciding; if uninformed, you do not. Within a round, wait time depends on earlier order decisions: a quote of X weeks means exactly X−1 earlier orders, though there may also have been earlier arrivals who chose not to order, and you do not observe your exact arrival rank. After all 4 decisions are made, production starts and delivery occurs in order of orders at one unit per week. You receive a fresh $4 endowment each round, round payoffs depend only on that round’s decision, wait time, and realized product value, and final payment is based on your average earnings across the 26 payoff-relevant rounds. A history box may show information from past rounds, but past rounds do not carry over into the current round’s queue or state.
