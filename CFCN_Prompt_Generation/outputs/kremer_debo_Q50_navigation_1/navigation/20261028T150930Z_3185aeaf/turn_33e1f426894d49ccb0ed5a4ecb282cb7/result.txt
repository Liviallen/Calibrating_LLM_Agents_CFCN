## Core decision-making structure in Q50
Consumers decide **whether to purchase or not** after observing:
- the **wait time** \(w\),
- their **information status** (in Q50, each consumer is informed with probability 0.5),
- and, if informed, the realized **product quality** (high or low).

The key logic from the theory and experiment is:
1. **Waiting is costly**.
2. **Informed consumers** can condition directly on true quality.
3. **Uninformed consumers** use **wait time as a signal** of likely quality, because in Q50 many earlier consumers may have been informed, so cumulative purchases embedded in wait time reveal information.

This is stated in the theory section on **pages 1–2**, where uninformed consumers infer quality from wait time because sales depend on quality through informed consumers, and in the experiment description on **pages 3–4**, where the Q50 treatment sets the informed-consumer probability to 50%.

# Number of mechanisms
**m = 3**

## Mechanism 1: Baseline wait-cost sensitivity
A consumer trades off product value against the disutility of waiting. This mechanism is always present in Q50 for all consumers.

**Levels of engagement**
- **Low**: weak sensitivity to additional wait time
- **High**: strong sensitivity to additional wait time

---

## Mechanism 2: Direct quality-contingent responding
This captures whether the consumer can respond directly to realized quality rather than relying only on prior beliefs or queue-based inference. In Q50, this varies by whether the consumer is informed.

**Levels of engagement**
- **Off**: consumer is uninformed and cannot observe realized quality directly
- **On**: consumer is informed and conditions choice on realized quality

---

## Mechanism 3: Social inference from wait time
This captures the distinct inferential use of wait time as information about quality. In Q50 this is central for uninformed consumers, because with 50% informed consumers, wait time is informative about earlier quality-based purchases.

**Levels of engagement**
- **Off**: little or no use of wait time as a signal of quality
- **On**: uses wait time to infer likely product quality from prior demand

# Why this is the minimal sufficient set
These three mechanisms are conceptually distinct and align with the paper’s Q50 logic:

- **Wait-cost sensitivity** is the basic economic tradeoff and is always present.
- **Direct quality observation** is a separate driver: informed consumers know quality and do not need to infer it.
- **Social inference from wait time** is the key observational-learning mechanism for uninformed consumers in Q50.

Variation is therefore **not just one continuum**. It combines:
- a common valuation-vs-wait tradeoff,
- a difference in information access,
- and a distinct inferential response to queue length / wait time.
